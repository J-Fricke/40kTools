# 40k v1.2 meta — competitive thoughts (cross-event synthesis)

Merges findings from all three events evaluated so far, all played under MFM
v1.2 (legal 2026-08-05): **WTC Warmaster GT 2026** (2026-08-11–13, 578
players, 55 five-plus-win lists studied in depth — see
`wtc-warmaster-2026-meta-notes.md`), **Warhammer Open Newport**
(2026-08-14–16, 188 players, 21 lists) and **The Upkeep Games GT I**
(2026-08-15–16, 126 players, 21 lists). Confirmed via `mfmdiff.com` that v1.2
changed zero points for Grey Knights/Votann/Custodes/Chaos Knights, so this is
a clean "day one of the new dataslate" read, not stale data.

**Methodology caveat, don't skip this**: Warmaster's 55 lists are filtered to
5+ wins only (strong signal — these are the actual winning builds). Newport
and Upkeep's 42 lists are *every* submitted list regardless of record (weaker
signal, more scattered) because far fewer players at those events uploaded
full lists. Treat Warmaster's archetype calls as "this is what's winning" and
the other two as "this is what's being played, some of it is winning."
Numbers are never blended across events into one stat — kept side by side.

## Threat classification: durability vs. tempo (read this before the archetype lists)

A unit landing on a "hardest to kill" list doesn't mean it's the scariest
thing on the table, and a unit that dies to a stiff breeze doesn't mean it's
not dangerous. These are two different axes and this doc (and the GK
matchplay reference) tries to keep them separate:

- **Durability threats** — need focused fire or a stratagem to remove in one
  activation; the danger is that they *don't die* and keep swinging back.
  Keeper of Secrets, Magnus the Red, Bloodcrushers, Custodes Allarus bricks.
- **Tempo/efficiency threats** — cheap, fast, often huge attack volume or
  first-strike-ish speed, but fold to almost anything. The danger is what
  they *do* before they die (tie up a unit, contest an objective, chew a
  weakened squad), not how hard they are to kill. **Daemonette squads are the
  clean example**: 9-10 models for ~90pts, T3/no armor save, dies to a light
  breeze (any single GK unit clears one in one activation) — but they're
  fast, cheap enough to field 5-7 of, and put out a large number of attacks.
  Their real threat is arithmetic (a lot of cheap efficient attacks arriving
  quickly), not survivability. They don't deserve a "boogeyman" writeup next
  to Keeper of Secrets — they deserve a "kill it if it's free, don't detour
  for it" note.

Applying this lens below instead of just "what's hard to remove."

## Per-disposition dominant archetypes (merged across all 3 events)

### Take and Hold
- **Ork "Ghazghkull + 19 Boyz" shell is the most repeated archetype found in
  this entire survey** — 5 of 7 Ork lists at Warmaster, plus 3 more
  independent players at Newport/Upkeep (8 total sightings across 3 events).
  Durability isn't the issue (any GK unit clears a Boyz blob); the problem is
  raw unit count contesting objectives faster than we can wipe them.
- **Space Marines "Librarius Conclave + Reclamation Force" melee deathball**
  — new pattern, not seen at Warmaster but 3 independent players at
  Newport/Upkeep (Captain Titus+Wardens of Ultramar+Bladeguard in one,
  Marneus Calgar+Cato Sicarius+Victrix Honour Guard+Sternguard in another).
  A durability threat — big multi-character melee bricks.
- Custodes recurs as a solid, non-dominant Take and Hold choice across both
  samples (3 lists at Warmaster, 1 at Newport).

### Reconnaissance (our disposition)
- **Dark Angels "Azrael + Lion El'Jonson speed force" is the solved list at
  Warmaster** (5 of 6 lists nearly identical) — Lion El'Jonson is a genuine
  durability threat (2+/4++ melee centerpiece), backed by 5-6 fast vehicles.
- **A possible "Imperium fast-vehicle Reconnaissance" pattern — one event,
  not yet confirmed cross-event**: 4 of Newport's 6 Reconnaissance lists
  (Dark Angels, Raven Guard, two plain SM builds) independently ran 2-3x
  Land Speeder + 2-3x Storm Speeder. Structurally similar to DA's own
  vehicle-heavy speed force. But Upkeep's 3 Reconnaissance lists (T'au,
  Custodes, Emperor's Children) had zero of this shell — so this reads as a
  cluster specific to one 188-player field (plausibly a local-scene/netlist-
  copy effect) rather than a confirmed format-wide trend. Needs a sighting
  at a third event before treating it as "expect this."
- **Chaos Daemons (Slaanesh)/Emperor's Children Reconnaissance runs the same
  Keeper of Secrets + Daemonettes + Defiler trio** seen under Priority Assets
  — a durability threat (Keeper) riding along with tempo threats (Daemonettes,
  see classification note above).
- T'au (Crisis-suit alpha-strike) and Drukhari (Lelith/Archon/Drazhar
  multi-Incubi+Wych blade-spam) are opposite-profile tempo/burst threats —
  neither is a durability problem, both are "kill our stuff before we act."

### Priority Assets
- **Chaos Daemons (Slaanesh) "Contorted Epitome + Daemonette/Fiends/Keeper"
  is the solved list at Warmaster** (5 of 5 lists). One real durability piece
  (Keeper) surrounded by tempo pieces (Daemonettes, Fiends) — see
  classification note. Real match data: the single worst GK result found in
  this whole survey (100-23 blowout) came from this archetype.
- Adeptus Mechanicus posted the best single result found in the newer data
  (8-0 at Newport, Lords of the Forge/Skitarii Hunter Cohort) — one result,
  not a pattern yet, but worth flagging as a faction to watch.
- T'au (Mont'ka) recurs as a strong, non-dominant choice.

### Purge the Foe
- **Chaos Daemons (Khorne) "Bloodcrusher" builds recur across multiple lists**
  at Warmaster (2 of 9, both top finishers) — a genuine durability threat
  (T7/3+/5++), distinct from the Slaanesh tempo-heavy build above.
- Otherwise genuinely mixed at every event studied — no single dominant
  archetype the way Take and Hold/Reconnaissance/Priority Assets each have
  one. Community's read of this as GK's favorable matchup remains untested
  by real match data (no Purge the Foe result surfaced for GK anywhere).

### Disruption
- **Consistently the least-played disposition at the top tables across all
  three events** — 1/55 at Warmaster, 1/42 at Newport+Upkeep combined. If
  you're prepping, this is the lowest-priority matchup by a wide margin.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Chaos Daemons is the single most format-warping faction across all
   three events**, full stop — dominant in Priority Assets (Slaanesh) *and*
   recurring strong in Purge the Foe (Khorne) *and* its Keeper/Daemonette/
   Defiler trio shows up again in Emperor's Children Reconnaissance. No other
   faction shows up as a top archetype in more than one disposition. If
   there's one faction worth a dedicated matchup plan beyond our own
   disposition, it's this one, in whichever flavor is in front of us.
2. **Two different winning philosophies coexist and both work**: "a small
   number of premium hard-to-kill pieces" (Keeper of Secrets, Magnus/Lord of
   Change, Lion El'Jonson, Custodes Allarus) vs. "overwhelming cheap body
   count" (Ork Boyz/Gretchin/Squighogs, Slaanesh Daemonette spam). Don't
   evaluate an opposing list's danger by durability alone — ask which
   philosophy it's running and answer accordingly (focus-fire + stratagems
   for the first, board-coverage/objective racing for the second).
3. **"Reconnaissance rewards speed" is confirmed for Dark Angels specifically
   (solved list, 5/6 at Warmaster) but only single-event evidence for it
   generalizing further** — the SM skimmer-spam cluster at Newport is
   suggestive, not confirmed (see caveat above: zero sightings at Upkeep).
   Worth re-checking at a third event before assuming the disposition itself
   pulls every faction toward fast vehicles. Relevant to us directly either
   way since it's our own disposition.

## Where Grey Knights fits

Reconnaissance is GK's best disposition in the cross-event aggregate (67%
win rate, n=12 — small but real, see `wtc-warmaster-2026-meta-notes.md` for
the full table). Our kill-tools (Abominus-Class Targets on BTS/Crowe+
Purifiers, never GMND) are now validated against three independent
durability-category targets (Keeper of Secrets, Bloodcrusher, Magnus the
Red) with consistent results. The open structural question — whether our
BTS+Purifier-anchored build or a Dreadknight/transport-heavy alternative is
the better shape — remains unresolved; no real GK list matching our exact
composition has turned up anywhere across all three events to check against
directly.
