## List lineage (why this isn't one fixed list, it's an evolving thesis)

1. **2026-07-20, Disruption (team-assigned), pre-MFM-v1.2**: Lib+10 paladins
   (66th, 5 psycannon), Crowe+10 purifiers (4 psycannon), 4 termies, 5 strikes,
   10 ints, 10 ints (T1 ingress), 5 ints (T1 ingress). Won with this shape.
2. **2026-07-24, tourney-eve tweak**: swapped down to Lib+5 paladins (66th,
   cannons) + added GMNDK+sigil, kept Crowe+10 cannons, 30 ints total, 5
   strikes, 5 purgation (psilencers) as points-filler, Rhino. Scodge's own
   caveat: didn't want to cut Crowe, GMNDK "felt valuable after test games."
3. **2026-07-27, post-patch, repurposed for Recon**: same shape played as
   Recon into an EC Priority-Assets opponent and a BA Take-and-Hold opponent —
   "both comfortable wins, don't think I want to change it yet."
4. **This eventually becomes the WTC Warmaster 4-2 result** (II+Sanctic
   Spearhead, 134/578) — see `wtc-warmaster-2026-meta-notes.md`.

Takeaway: both the 10-Paladin brick (step 1) and the 5-Paladin+GMNDK brick
(steps 2-4) won real games for the same author in the same short window.
Neither is proven better — there's genuine precedent for both, so don't treat
"cut to 5 Paladins" as a forced correction if you want the 10-brick in.

## Disposition framework (conceptual, not composition-dependent — won't go stale)

- **Disruption**: "Warhammer harder and better" — deny the opponent's ability
  to match you on primary.
- **Priority Assets**: aggressive push for control of the board area holding
  the last marker after a mostly one-sided mid-board action dance.
- **Purge the Foe**: play uninteractive 40k — deny/hold more, don't lose
  units, still generate kills.
- **Reconnaissance**: superficially lopsided in the Recon player's favor, but
  the real win condition is forcing the opponent to run out of scoring units
  early via aggressive pushes into staging areas that clear/block their
  objective-stealers.
- **Take and Hold**: both players can near-max primary playing safe (T&H
  player's job is just holding a triangle for 5 turns) — you need a secondary
  differential, since T&H is tied to killing on/near objectives. Only really
  punishing on Dawn of War; the other two T&H missions are "6/turn + kills, at
  minimum a draw if you control where they die."
- **Mirror (Recon v Recon)**: no clever answer — "just send it and try to stop
  them maxing primary."
- Cross-check from Aneruen (Votann perspective): Purge and Disruption are
  "straight up favoured for Recon"; PA is "a great 10-10 dispo... the Votann
  special" — independently consistent with scodge's read.
- What building for Disruption/Recon actually demands of a list: kill
  effectively, move fast, score secondaries well, action all over the board,
  don't die, project credible threat everywhere. Candidate archetypes that
  satisfy this: Crowe+Purifiers, Dreadknights, Land Raider Redeemer, Paladin
  bricks, Strikes/MSU Terminators, Interceptors.

## Argent Assault vs. Immaterial Interdiction — the core detachment argument

Why NOT Argent Assault, despite it looking like an obvious fit for Paladin
bricks: in 10th, coherency rules + legal rapid-redeploy let you throw bricks
deep and reload via Mists. Both are gone/nerfed in 11th — to emulate the old
pattern now you need two bricks and still can't push as far. AA's actual buffs
(+1 to wound, precision, +1 to charge) don't move the needle much on a single
10-man brick that already kills almost anything without them — the wound
reroll in particular is wasted on a unit that isn't struggling to kill things.
**AA benefits MSU/8-man Paladins far more than 10-mans**; for a single-brick
list, the 1DP is better spent elsewhere.

Why Immaterial Interdiction: Disruption/Recon demand handling a wide spread of
possible plays with real action-economy cost, and 500-600pt bricks can't
multitask. The actual pattern that emerged: commit the brick(s) late-game once
a clear path opens, while cheap units (Interceptors) harvest chaff/MEQ, action,
and trade in the meantime. II's kit maps directly onto that:
- **By Thought Alone**: the stormbolter shots are near-irrelevant; the real
  value is completing an instant action then moving (e.g. Plunder, or Booby
  Trap on the Death Trap T&H mission).
- **Blades from Beyond**: Lethal + Sustained with Lance.
- **Responsive Displacement**: extra reactive moves, reduces pressure on Mists.
- **Echojump**: free advance-and-charge-with-guardrails, usable on multiple
  units at once, doesn't stop you shooting/actioning — also a way to dodge
  cheap Heroic Intervention.
- Note this matches `greyknights-recon-matchplay-reference.md`'s existing
  finding that II has zero kill-boosting tools (all Interceptor-only
  mobility/action-economy) — same conclusion, different angle.

This is the same thesis scodge was circling when he later floated swapping
Sanctic Spearhead for Banishers+II after the WTC result — not a new doubt,
a continuation of a position held since July.

## Anti-tank doctrine — don't lean on Terminators for it

Both scodge and Aneruen independently tested Terminator bricks vs. armor and
came away unimpressed: Aneruen's 10-man-with-Lance test "gets a rhino pretty
often, that's about the extent." Scodge's conclusion: don't expect real
vehicle-killing from Terminators in melee — treat them as "a layer of
assurance after shooting, at most." Scodge also flags not trusting 4-man
Terminator squads to survive at all ("I do not trust 4 mans to live") —
bricking to 8-10 or not taking them.

**The actual anti-armor plan**: GMNDK + the Lance/Lethal-Sustained stratagem
does the real work; Crowe covers chaos matchups specifically. Against
genuinely tank/melee-monster-heavy lists, the doctrine is explicitly **"you
can just hide"** — cycle Interceptors out via Mists and rapid-deploy the next
one rather than brawling. This is the direct answer to the EC daemon
prince/defiler worry scodge raised separately: positioning to avoid the fight,
not more gun, is the counter.

## Paladin Squad vs. Brotherhood Terminator Squad — correcting a recurring mix-up

Not from the Discord thread — a datasheet check (2026-08-24) prompted by user
recurring confusion ("I keep counting BTS as helping with monsters"). Worth
recording so it doesn't resurface. Source: `ref/greyknights-10th-datasheets.txt`.

**BTS does NOT have a vehicle/monster bonus.** Its inherent ability, Force
Edge, reads: "Each time a model in this unit makes a melee attack that
targets a unit **(excluding MONSTERS and VEHICLES)**, improve the Armour
Penetration characteristic of that attack by 1." That's an anti-infantry/
anti-character AP bump (-2→-3), explicitly excluding the exact target type
it gets credited for helping with. The actual source of the confusion:
`greyknights-recon-matchplay-reference.md`'s finding that the *Sanctic
Spearhead stratagem* Abominus-Class Targets (+1 to wound vs Monster/Vehicle,
usable on any unit, 1CP) is best spent on BTS rather than GMND — that's a
stratagem choice about where to spend a CP, not a BTS datasheet trait.

Base melee profile is identical between the two (Nemesis force weapon, A4 S6
AP-2 D2). The real differences:

| | Paladin Squad | Brotherhood Terminator Squad |
|---|---|---|
| WS | 2+ | 3+ |
| Inherent melee bonus | Attuned Onslaught: +1D, but only until end of the turn it charges — **no target-type exclusion** | Force Edge: +1 AP, always on, **excludes Monster/Vehicle** |
| OC | 1 | 2 |
| 10-model cost | 450 | 375 |

**Against a vehicle/monster specifically, Paladins are just better, no
asterisk**: better WS, and the charge-turn +1D still applies (no exclusion),
while BTS gets nothing extra. The 16.7-point hit-rate swing from WS2+ vs 3+
(66.7%→83.3%) is the whole story for that matchup — there's no offsetting BTS
advantage to weigh against it.

Where BTS actually earns its points isn't melee math against hard targets at
all — it's 75pts cheaper at 10 models and OC2 vs OC1. Given the standing
finding elsewhere in these notes that GK's real structural problem is
unit-count/coverage, not lethality, that's a genuine case for BTS as a
cheaper objective-holding body rather than a combat piece — a coverage/points
tradeoff, not a hidden anti-armor angle.

`project_greyknights_recon_list`). Good unprompted cross-check, not something
