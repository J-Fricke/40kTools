# 40k v1.2 meta — competitive thoughts (cross-event synthesis)

Merges findings from six events, all played under MFM v1.2 (legal
2026-08-05). `mfmdiff.com` confirms v1.2 changed zero points for Grey
Knights/Votann/Custodes/Chaos Knights, so this is a clean read for our own
list-building, not stale data.

**Methodology, read before anything else**: only two of these six
(Warmaster, HUXLEYS OPEN) are genuinely representative of their whole
field — the other four looked unfiltered at first (no min-wins applied) but
turned out to be self-selected toward winners once the actual record
distributions were checked (players who do well share their list far more
often than players who don't at smaller events). Treat the two full-field
events as the only sources for "what's not working" comparisons; treat all
six as comparable to each other for "what's winning," roughly the same way
Warmaster's own top-tier-only slice (5+ wins, n=55: Take and Hold 27%,
Priority Assets 27%, Reconnaissance 27%, Purge the Foe 16%, Disruption 2%)
is used as the baseline the four winner-skewed events get compared against.
Numbers are never blended across events into one stat — kept side by side.

## The headline finding from this round: two full-field samples disagree

Warmaster (full field, n=587) showed Reconnaissance's disposition share
climbing steadily from 17% at the bottom performance tier to 27% at the
top, and Dark Angels holding 40% of the entire Reconnaissance top tier.
**HUXLEYS OPEN (full field, n=99) shows close to the opposite on both
counts**: Reconnaissance's share is *higher* at the bottom (23%) than the
top (9%), and Dark Angels' five entries there produced zero top-tier
finishes (best result 3-3, with the exact same "Darkflight Pursuit |
Company of Hunters" shell that went 5/6 at Warmaster). Both samples are
small once you isolate Reconnaissance specifically (Warmaster: 116 Recon
lists across all tiers; HUXLEYS OPEN: 11), so neither one should be treated
as settled - **the honest state of the evidence is "we don't yet know
whether Reconnaissance is a genuinely strong disposition to build around,
or whether Warmaster's result was event-specific."** This directly affects
how much weight to put on our own disposition choice being data-backed
versus just a defensible one - see "Where Grey Knights fits" below for how
this changes that framing.

## Per-disposition dominant archetypes (merged across all six events)

### Take and Hold
- **Ork "Ghazghkull + 19 Boyz" shell is the most repeated archetype found in
  this entire survey** — 5 of 7 Ork lists at Warmaster, plus independent
  players at Newport, Upkeep, and HUXLEYS OPEN (9+ total sightings across
  four events now). Durability isn't the issue (any GK unit clears a Boyz
  blob); the problem is raw unit count contesting objectives faster than we
  can wipe them.
- **Space Marines "Librarius Conclave + Reclamation Force" melee deathball**
  — 3 independent players across Newport and Upkeep (Captain Titus+Wardens
  of Ultramar+Bladeguard in one, Marneus Calgar+Cato Sicarius+Victrix Honour
  Guard+Sternguard in another). Not seen in either full-field event's own
  top tier (Warmaster or HUXLEYS OPEN) - still a real pattern at the two
  events it appeared at, but its absence from both larger samples means
  it's not yet confirmed as a *dominant* shell, just a recurring one.
- Custodes recurs as a solid, non-dominant Take and Hold choice across
  multiple samples.

### Reconnaissance (our disposition)
- **Dark Angels "Azrael + Lion El'Jonson speed force"**: 5/6 at Warmaster,
  2/3 (both 4-1) at Edmonton - but 0/5 top-tier and mediocre records (3-3,
  2-2) at HUXLEYS OPEN. Genuinely mixed evidence now across three sightings
  with results, not a settled "this always wins" archetype.
- **T'au "Kauyon" Crisis-suit alpha-strike is a new, well-supported
  pattern**: 6-0 at Upkeep and 6-0 at Smite Club Open, both running the
  identical Experimental Prototype Cadre \| Kauyon detachment pairing. Two
  independent 6-0 results with the same shell at two different events is a
  stronger signal than anything else found for this disposition outside of
  Warmaster's own Dark Angels result.
- **The SM "skimmer spam" cluster is Newport-specific, not a real
  cross-event pattern** — 4 of Newport's 6 Reconnaissance lists ran
  2-3x Land Speeder + 2-3x Storm Speeder, but zero sightings at Upkeep,
  HUXLEYS OPEN, Smite Club Open, or Edmonton (11+ other Reconnaissance
  lists checked). Downgraded from "possible trend" to "one event's cluster."
- **Chaos Daemons (Slaanesh)/Emperor's Children Reconnaissance runs the same
  Keeper of Secrets + Daemonettes + Defiler trio** as Priority Assets, and
  the exact "Frenzied Host \| Carnival of Excess" detachment pairing repeats
  at both Upkeep (4-2) and Smite Club Open (4-2) - a durability threat
  (Keeper) riding along with tempo threats (Daemonettes, see classification
  note below).
- T'au (Crisis-suit alpha-strike) and Drukhari (Lelith/Archon/Drazhar
  multi-Incubi+Wych blade-spam) remain opposite-profile tempo/burst threats.

### Priority Assets
- **Chaos Daemons (Slaanesh) "Contorted Epitome + Daemonette/Fiends/Keeper"
  was the solved list at Warmaster** (5/5 lists there) but Chaos Daemons
  posted an unremarkable spread at HUXLEYS OPEN (0 top, 2 upper-mid, 2 mid,
  2 lower-mid, 0 bottom, out of 6 total) - not dominant at every event, just
  at Warmaster specifically. The single worst GK result found in this whole
  survey (100-23 blowout) still came from this archetype at Warmaster.
- Adeptus Mechanicus posted the best single result found in the newer data
  (8-0 at Newport, Lords of the Forge/Skitarii Hunter Cohort) - didn't
  reach HUXLEYS OPEN's top tier at all (no AdMech entries placed there).
  One strong result, not a confirmed pattern.
- T'au (Mont'ka) and Emperor's Children (Coterie of the Conceited, 2
  independent 5-1s at HUXLEYS OPEN) both recur as strong, non-dominant
  choices.

### Purge the Foe
- **Chaos Daemons (Khorne) "Bloodcrusher" builds recur across multiple
  Warmaster lists** (2 of 9, both top finishers) — a genuine durability
  threat (T7/3+/5++), distinct from the Slaanesh tempo-heavy build above.
  Not confirmed at other events (Smite Club Open's Purge the Foe entries
  were Chaos Space Marines/Orks/Necrons/Blood Angels, no Bloodcrushers).
- Otherwise genuinely mixed at every event studied — no single dominant
  archetype the way Take and Hold/Reconnaissance/Priority Assets each have
  candidates for one. Community's read of this as GK's favorable matchup
  remains untested by real match data (no Purge the Foe result surfaced for
  GK anywhere across all six events).

### Disruption
- **Consistently the least-played disposition at the top tables across
  every event checked** — 1/55 at Warmaster, 0% at HUXLEYS OPEN's top tier,
  0-15% everywhere else. If you're prepping, this is the lowest-priority
  matchup by a wide margin, full stop, six events in.

## Threat classification: durability vs. tempo (unchanged, still holds)

A unit landing on a "hardest to kill" list doesn't mean it's the scariest
thing on the table, and a unit that dies to a stiff breeze doesn't mean it's
not dangerous. These are two different axes:

- **Durability threats** — need focused fire or a stratagem to remove in one
  activation; the danger is that they *don't die* and keep swinging back.
  Keeper of Secrets, Magnus the Red, Bloodcrushers, Custodes Allarus bricks.
- **Tempo/efficiency threats** — cheap, fast, often huge attack volume or
  first-strike-ish speed, but fold to almost anything. **Daemonette squads
  are the clean example**: 9-10 models for ~90pts, T3/no armor save, dies to
  a light breeze — but fast, cheap enough to field 5-7 of, and a large
  attack volume. Their real threat is arithmetic, not survivability. They
  don't deserve a "boogeyman" writeup next to Keeper of Secrets — they
  deserve a "kill it if it's free, don't detour for it" note.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Chaos Daemons is still the faction most worth a dedicated matchup
   plan, but "format-warping at every event" was an overclaim** — dominant
   in Priority Assets and recurring strong in Purge the Foe *at Warmaster*,
   with its Keeper/Daemonette/Defiler trio showing up again in
   Emperor's Children Reconnaissance at two other events - but unremarkable
   at HUXLEYS OPEN specifically. Read as "the scariest version of this
   faction is real and worth prepping for," not "this faction wins
   everywhere."
2. **Two different winning philosophies coexist and both work**: "a small
   number of premium hard-to-kill pieces" (Keeper of Secrets, Magnus/Lord of
   Change, Lion El'Jonson, Custodes Allarus) vs. "overwhelming cheap body
   count" (Ork Boyz/Gretchin/Squighogs, Slaanesh Daemonette spam). Ask which
   philosophy an opposing list is running before judging its danger, rather
   than reading durability alone.
3. **"Reconnaissance rewards speed" no longer looks like a safe
   generalization** — see the headline finding above. Dark Angels' own
   result is now mixed rather than uniformly dominant, and the "SM skimmer
   spam" idea didn't survive a second look at more events. What *did* gain
   real support this round is T'au's Kauyon shell (2 independent 6-0s) and
   the Slaanesh Recon trio (2 independent 4-2s with the identical
   detachment) - specific archetypes, not a disposition-wide speed
   principle.

## Where Grey Knights fits

**This needs a more cautious framing than earlier drafts of this doc gave
it.** Reconnaissance is still GK's best disposition in the cross-event GK
win-rate aggregate (67%, n=12 — see `wtc-warmaster-2026-meta-notes.md`), and
nothing here contradicts that GK-specific number directly. But the broader
claim this doc previously leaned on — "Reconnaissance is a disposition that
rewards good play/speed in general, so building for it is a sound
strategic bet independent of our own small sample" — is weaker than it
looked before HUXLEYS OPEN's full-field data came in, since that pattern
didn't replicate. **Our reasons for choosing Reconnaissance now rest more
on our own settled preference and the II+Sanctic-specific real result
(scodge, 4-2) than on a general "Recon is a winning disposition" claim** -
which is a fine reason to keep going, just a different and more honest one
than before.

Our kill-tools (Abominus-Class Targets on BTS/Crowe+Purifiers, never GMND)
are validated against three independent durability-category targets
(Keeper of Secrets, Bloodcrusher, Magnus the Red) with consistent results -
this part of the analysis is unaffected by the disposition-level question
above. The open structural question also remains: whether our
BTS+Purifier-anchored build or a Dreadknight/transport-heavy alternative is
the better shape - no real GK list matching our exact composition has
turned up anywhere across all six events to check against directly.
